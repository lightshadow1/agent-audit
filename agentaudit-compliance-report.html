Critical Gaps & Remediation Plan

Prioritized by enforcement deadline and severity. Address critical gaps first — these represent the highest regulatory and financial risk.

CRITICAL AI System Inventory & Risk Classification (EU AI Act Art. 6 + ISO 42001 6.1)

No formal AI system registry exists. Cannot classify systems as prohibited, high-risk, limited-risk, or minimal-risk. 83% of enterprises share this gap — but it blocks all other compliance activities.

Remediation Steps
  • Create an AI system inventory cataloging all production agents with purpose, data sources, and deployment context
  • Classify each system using Annex III criteria (employment, credit, education, law enforcement = high-risk)
  • Document risk assessments with methodology, thresholds, and rationale
  • Publish model cards for each system (required by OMB M-26-04 for federal contracts)
CRITICAL Training Data Governance (EU AI Act Art. 10 + Colorado AI Act + NIST MAP)

No training data provenance, validation, or lineage tracking found. 78% of enterprises cannot validate training data. This is the single largest compliance exposure across all frameworks.

Remediation Steps
  • Implement data lineage tracking for all training datasets (source, transformations, validation status)
  • Add pre-pipeline validation checkpoints with documented acceptance criteria
  • Create data removal / right-to-erasure procedures with audit trails
  • Document training data sources for Colorado/Texas/California disclosure requirements
CRITICAL Log Retention (6 months) (Art. 26 — EU AI Act)

Trace data spans 90 days. Article 26 requires minimum 180 days (6 months) of log retention for deployers.

Remediation Steps
  • Configure your observability tool to retain data for at least 6 months
  • Langfuse Cloud: check your plan's retention limits
  • Self-hosted: configure database retention policies and verify with automated checks
  • For financial services: align with your existing record-keeping obligations (often 5–7 years)
CRITICAL Bias Testing & Third-Party Audit (NIST MEASURE + SOC 2 + Colorado AI Act)

No bias testing or fairness evaluation artifacts found. Auditors now reject self-certification — independent third-party bias audits are mandatory with specific statistical methodologies documented.

Remediation Steps
  • Engage a third-party auditor for bias and fairness testing (self-assessment is no longer accepted)
  • Document testing methodology, thresholds, and statistical approaches used
  • Store audit artifacts as immutable records linked to model versions
  • Schedule recurring audits (quarterly recommended for high-risk systems)
CRITICAL Consumer Notification & Opt-Out (Colorado AI Act + EU AI Act Art. 50)

No consumer disclosure events or opt-out mechanisms found. Colorado requires notification when AI is involved in consequential decisions. EU Art. 50 requires disclosure of AI-generated content.

Remediation Steps
  • Implement consumer notification at point of AI-assisted decision
  • Add opt-out pathway events to your agent workflow (route to human alternative)
  • Log all disclosure and opt-out events for audit trail
  • For EU: ensure AI-generated content is marked per Article 50 transparency requirements
WARNING Human Oversight Logging (Art. 14 — EU AI Act + CC5.3 — SOC 2)

Only 15 of 50 traces (30%) contain human oversight events. Article 14 requires human intervention logging for high-risk AI. Shallow click-through approval workflows don't constitute meaningful oversight per regulators.

Remediation Steps
  • Add human-in-the-loop checkpoints using LangGraph interrupt_before/interrupt_after on critical decision nodes
  • In Langfuse: use trace.event(name='human_oversight', metadata={reviewer_id, decision, policy_ref})
  • Ensure every high-risk decision path includes a substantive human review gate (not rubber-stamp)
  • Document your human oversight policy and reference it in trace metadata
WARNING Tamper Evidence / Log Integrity (EN 18229-1 + SOC 2)

Only 30 of 50 traces (60%) have hash-chain integrity verification. Auditors now require immutable, signed audit trails — data-layer enforcement, not just append-only semantics.

Remediation Steps
  • Enable hash-chain logging across all traces, not just a subset
  • Export traces to an append-only store (e.g., S3 with Object Lock, Azure Immutable Blob)
  • Add cryptographic signatures to trace batches for non-repudiation
  • Implement periodic integrity verification checks with alerting
WARNING Change Management (Version Logging) (CC8.1 — SOC 2 + ISO 42001 8.1)

Model and agent version logged inconsistently. Full version lineage needed for rollback procedures and model governance documentation.

Remediation Steps
  • Log agent_version and model version in every trace event metadata
  • Maintain a version registry with deployment authorization records
  • Document rollback procedures for each agent version
WARNING Output Completeness (PI1.4 — SOC 2)

Only 135 of 303 events (44%) include output data. Processing integrity criteria require comprehensive output logging for auditability.

Remediation Steps
  • Ensure all LLM calls, tool calls, and decision nodes log their output data
  • For sensitive outputs: log a hash or redacted version if full content cannot be stored
  • Add output validation checkpoints to verify processing integrity
